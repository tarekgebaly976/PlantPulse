# PlantPulse cartoon – from a noise to a closed work order

| Scene | Shown | Narration (one line per step) |
|---|---|---|
| 01-report | anim | Every job starts with a problem. Here, the operator hears a noise on the press section.<br>He reports it from the phone or the computer: a work request, with the equipment and what he sees.<br>The supervisor sees new requests first, with breakdowns marked. He accepts it, and accepting creates the work order. |
| 02-plan | anim | The new work order starts in triage. The planner sets the dates, the estimated hours and the priority.<br>And the safety needs: lock-out tag-out, a permit, or a test after the work.<br>The supervisor approves it and assigns a technician. If he is on leave, already booked, or missing a required skill, PlantPulse says so first. |
| 03-work | anim | With lock-out signed off, the technician starts the work on the phone.<br>He follows the checklist, books his hours, and records the spare parts he fitted. Hours and parts are costed at once.<br>Then the findings, the root cause and the downtime. The lock comes off, and the job goes for review. |
| 04-close | anim | The supervisor reviews it. If something is missing, like the failure codes, PlantPulse says exactly what.<br>With the problem, cause and remedy codes in place, it is completed, and then closed.<br>The history keeps every step with who and when. The work order shows its hours, parts and total cost, and the PDF report puts it all on one page.<br>Closed work counts in the KPIs. And a repeated failure is proposed for root cause analysis. |
| 05-end | anim | That is the life of a work order, from the first noise to the KPIs. Next: preventive maintenance. |

255 words, about 102 s of voice at a calm pace.
