# PlantPulse cartoon – shifts, handover, skills and the weekly schedule

| Scene | Shown | Narration (one line per step) |
|---|---|---|
| 01-roster | anim | Maintenance runs around the clock, every day of the week. Teams follow a rotation pattern, and the roster is made from it.<br>Leave, overtime and call-outs are entered on the roster too.<br>Manpower turns it into the hours available each day, and flags a day with planned work but nobody rostered. |
| 02-handover | anim | At the end of the shift, the outgoing supervisor writes the handover: the summary, breakdowns still open, pending work, safety concerns, open permits and critical risks.<br>The incoming supervisor reads it and confirms it, and then it is locked. Nobody can confirm their own handover. |
| 03-skills | anim | The engineer keeps the skill matrix: one row per person, one column per skill, at three levels, from basic to expert.<br>Certificates have an expiry date. Green is valid, amber expires within thirty days, red has expired.<br>Each skill shows how many people are qualified. Red means nobody, amber means just one person.<br>Work orders say which skills they need. Assigning someone without them, or with an expired certificate, gives a warning first. |
| 04-week | anim | Every week, the planner presses Propose schedule. The optimiser places the approved work on the people at work, matching trades, skills, valid certificates, and a supervisor on shift for LOTO.<br>Work that needs the line stopped only goes into a line stop window agreed with production. What does not fit is deferred, and each one says why.<br>The planner can move a job by hand. Then the engineer approves the week, and the manager gives the final approval.<br>The planned dates and the technicians are written into the work orders, and each technician sees the week in My work. |
| 05-end | anim | The right people, with the right skills, on the right day. Next: shutdowns, the big planned stops. |
