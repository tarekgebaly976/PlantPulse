# PlantPulse cartoon – condition monitoring and the Nelson rules

| Scene | Shown | Narration (one line per step) |
|---|---|---|
| 01-points | anim | Machines warn us before they break: vibration, temperature, current. Condition monitoring listens to them.<br>Each measurement point has a technique, a unit, and two limits: warning and alarm.<br>Readings are entered on site, from a round, or received from plant systems. Reading rounds become work orders, just like preventive maintenance. |
| 02-reading | anim | On the round, the technician measures the drive motor bearing. Recording a reading takes one number.<br>The answer comes at once: the warning limit is passed. An alert is raised, and the counter on the dashboard goes up. A plain limit check: nothing is predicted. |
| 03-chart | anim | The engineer opens the point's control chart: the last hundred readings, with the average, the control limits, and the warning and alarm limits.<br>The eight Nelson rules find patterns, like a steady rise, even inside the limits. Here, seven points in a row are rising. This change is real. |
| 04-rules | anim | So what are the Nelson rules? Each chart has a centre line, the average, and a sigma, worked out from how much each reading moves from the one before. A limit only speaks when it is already late. The Nelson rules look for patterns.<br>Rule one: one point more than three sigma from the centre line, often a new person or a wrong setup. Rule two: seven points in a row on the same side of the line: the setup or the process has changed.<br>Rule three: seven points in a row, all rising or all falling. That is a trend, like wear. Rule four: fourteen points in a row going up and down, in turn. That is over-control: someone adjusting too often.<br>Rule five: two out of three points more than two sigma out, on the same side, again a new person or a wrong setup. Rule six: four out of five points more than one sigma out: a small shift.<br>Rule seven: fourteen points in a row hugging the centre line, within one sigma. Rule eight: eight points in a row more than one sigma away, on either side. Both say the process has changed. |
| 05-where | anim | Where does PlantPulse use them? First, in condition monitoring. Every measurement point has its control chart, and the points list has a Nelson column, worked out from the last thirty readings, so a pattern shows even inside the limits.<br>Second, on Home. The condition monitoring section lists the points in alarm, or with a Nelson signal, most urgent first.<br>Third, in company KPIs. Each KPI has a control chart over the months, against its target, with the same eight rules and the probable cause of each.<br>And the management presentation counts the points with a Nelson signal, so the monthly review sees them too. |
| 06-fix | anim | The engineer acknowledges the alert, and creates a predictive work order with one click.<br>The bearing is changed in a planned stop, not a breakdown. The alert is resolved with what was found, and the readings go back to normal.<br>Points, rounds and the history of past readings can be imported from Excel, so the trends start full, and a priority list sets which points matter most. |
| 07-end | anim | Listen to the machines, and fix them before they stop. That is condition monitoring in PlantPulse. |
